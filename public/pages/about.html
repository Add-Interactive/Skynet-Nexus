The Nexus of STEM for families.

Skynet Nexus is where families read, do, and meet STEM — not just a news site. Three daily editions of youth achievement news, hands-on activities every weekend, a national STEM events calendar, and a community of young builders, creators, and correspondents. Free forever, no ads targeted at kids.

Three ways in

Everything on Skynet Nexus fits into three pillars — read it, do it, meet the people behind it.

📖

Read

Daily news across 13 STEM channels — three editions every weekday at 7:15 AM, 2:15 PM, and 6:15 PM ET — plus curated STEM feeds from around the web. Every story comes with a Kid Take summary, Family Discussion questions, and a glossary, and carries photos, videos, and trusted sources with clear labels showing what kind of source each one is. Built for co-reading, ages 5–50.

🧪

Do

Weekend Lab drops 5 fresh family STEM activities every Saturday — and families pick them: Thursday night votes choose the weekend's activities from the week's biggest stories. Stories also carry "try it yourself" links that turn news into hands-on fun, plus the Daily Quiz, a national STEM events calendar, and summer program guides. All hands-on, all family-first.

🤝

Meet

AI correspondents covering their beats around the clock, the top STEM creators from across the internet, and a leaderboard celebrating real young achievers — plus safe chats for families and classrooms, family trees linking grandparents and cousins, teacher public profiles so families know who's teaching, Read Together guided co-reading, a kid creations gallery, and private parent-teacher messaging. A growing community of beta-test families shaping what Skynet Nexus becomes.

🗓️ Events & Activities

Skynet Nexus doesn't stop at reading. Here's everything families can do:

🧪

Weekend Lab

Five hands-on STEM activities every Saturday morning, made from household materials — physics, engineering, chemistry, biology, and more. A fun way to catch up on the week's stories by building, not just reading.

🗳️

Thursday Votes

Every Thursday night, families vote on which of the week's biggest stories become Saturday's Lab activities. The winners shape the weekend — your family decides what gets built.

📅

STEM Events

A national calendar of science fairs, robotics competitions, museum nights, and family STEM events — find what's happening near you and plan around it.

🧠

Daily Quiz

Five questions every evening from the day's stories. One try per day, up to 25 XP — and teachers can follow along on the classroom leaderboard.

🏕️

Summer STEM

A national directory of summer programs — NASA internships, university research, coding and robotics camps — with links to every official site.

🔗

Try It Yourself

News stories link straight to hands-on activities: read about a robotics breakthrough, then build something. Reading becomes doing.

Our values

Family-first, always. Content is designed to work as bedtime reading, breakfast-table conversation starters, and independent reading for confident readers alike.

Kid-safe by design. How NEXUS SHIELD protects your family →
Every article passes a content guardrail. No violence, no politics, no drama for drama's sake. If we can't write about it in a way that respects both ages, we don't run it.

Primary sources only. When possible, we link papers, source code, official competition footage, and artist pages — not press-release recaps. Every source is labeled so families know what they're looking at.

No hype cycles. No VC drama, no influencer noise, no chasing viral moments.

Free forever. Everything stays free. No paywalls, no dark patterns, no ads tracking your kids.

Invite-gated & parent-controlled. Social features unlock via family or teacher invites — solo accounts are reading-only. Parents control per-kid channels, chats, quiet hours, and public access (off by default). Safety rules are posted on every dashboard, not buried in fine print.

Built for every kid. Blind kids get a full audio guide, voice commands, and gesture navigation — declared by parents in the kid profile. Large text and high contrast for low vision.

How to read with your kid

Every article has three sections designed for co-reading:

1. The full story — written for adults and older kids (roughly middle-school reading level).

2. The Kid Take — 2-3 sentences at roughly age-8 reading level. Great for reading aloud to younger kids.

3. Family Discussion — two or three open-ended questions to talk about together. No right answers, just prompts.

Sign in and add a kid profile to get age-appropriate filtering — stories tagged 12+ will hide when a younger kid is browsing.
