💥 THE KESSLER PROBLEM

Imagine one crash in space shattering a satellite into a thousand pieces — and each piece smashing another satellite. Scientists call this chain reaction the Kessler syndrome: dominoes in orbit! The orange dots are real tracked junk: dead rocket stages and shattered bits. That's why old satellites are steered to burn up safely, or parked in graveyard orbits.

THE NEIGHBORHOODS

🛰️ LEO — Low Earth Orbit 200 – 2,000 km

The busy downtown! The ISS, China's Tiangong station, the Hubble telescope, Starlink's thousands of internet satellites, and most Earth-photo satellites all crowd in here. It's close, so cameras see Earth in amazing detail and signals are fast — but air drag means satellites need rocket boosts to stay up, and it's getting crowded.

🧭 MEO — Medium Earth Orbit 2,000 – 35,786 km

The navigation highway. GPS (American), Galileo (European) and GLONASS (Russian) satellites live around 20,000 km up, where each one can see a huge area of Earth. Your phone figures out where you are by timing signals from at least four of them!

📡 GEO — Geostationary Orbit 35,786 km

The magic parking ring! At exactly 35,786 km above the equator, a satellite circles Earth at the same speed Earth spins — so it hovers over one spot forever. TV satellites beam your shows down from here, and weather satellites stare at the same storms all day. There's only ONE such ring, so parking spots are precious.

🌀 HEO — Highly Elliptical Orbit

The stretched loops! Some satellites fly long oval orbits that swoop low and swing far out — like Russia's Molniya satellites, which hang high over the North Pole for hours to serve places GEO satellites can't see well.

🪦 Graveyard orbit ~300 km above GEO

Space's retirement home. When a GEO satellite runs out of fuel, it can't come home (it's far too high to fall back). Instead it's pushed ~300 km higher into a quiet zone where it will drift, harmless, for thousands of years.

WHO DECIDES WHO PARKS WHERE?

🅿️ The ITU hands out parking spots. The International Telecommunication Union (part of the United Nations) assigns slots on the GEO ring plus the radio frequencies each satellite may use — like numbered parking spaces, because two satellites too close together would shout over each other on the radio!

📜 Countries license launches. In the US, the FCC (and others) must approve every launch and every radio signal. Other countries have their own space agencies doing the same job.

📋 Everyone registers. By international treaty, every country logs its satellites with UNOOSA, the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs — a public guest-book for everything humans put in orbit.

⚠️ You can't just launch anywhere because orbits are shared roads: a wrong move risks smashing into someone else's satellite or adding to the cloud of space junk.

WHAT HAPPENS AT THE END?

🔥 Low satellites burn up. Old LEO satellites are steered into the atmosphere, where they vaporize in a fiery streak — the cleanest goodbye.

🪦 High satellites move out. GEO satellites too high to fall back get pushed to the graveyard orbit instead.

💥 Kessler syndrome. Scientists worry that if junk keeps piling up, one crash could shatter satellites into thousands of pieces, causing more crashes in a chain reaction — like dominoes in space. That's why cleaning up and careful parking matter!
