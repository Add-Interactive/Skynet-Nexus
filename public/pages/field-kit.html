📐 Mission 1 · The Pendulum Test (Gus)

Can your phone measure gravity?

  1. Predict: a longer pendulum swings slower or faster?
  2. Scan: hold your phone gently, swing it like a pendulum, run Motion Lab for 10 swings. Count the glowing waves on the graph!
  3. Explain: time ÷ swings = your period. Did it match your prediction?

🔊 Mission 2 · Sound Map of Your World (Cadence)

Where is your world loudest — and quietest?

  1. Predict: rank 4 places from quietest to loudest before you measure.
  2. Scan: run Sound Lab in each place for 10 seconds and log the peak.
  3. Explain: compare your logs with your prediction. Any surprises?

🧭 Mission 3 · The North Hunt (Rowan)

Can you navigate by instrument alone?

  1. Predict: point at where you think north is. No peeking!
  2. Scan: start the Geo Compass — the amber line tells you live how many degrees you are from true north.
  3. Explain: how far off were you? Log your true-north find.

🔒 FOR GUARDIANS & TEACHERS

The Field Kit uses only the sensors a learner taps “Start” on, explains each permission in plain language first, and stores scan logs in the browser on this device. It never records audio, never uploads photos, and never stores location. Scan logs stay on this device unless the cadet deliberately taps Share to send one scan to their own classroom or family crew — those are the only places Crew Link exists. The Astro Scanner shows a position fix on screen only, and the compass shows a heading, not a position. Every display on this page is driven by live sensor data; decorative animation is used only for the device frame itself, never to imitate a reading. Phone sensors are teaching-grade, not lab-grade: missions are built around comparisons and patterns, and learning that every instrument has error is part of the science.
