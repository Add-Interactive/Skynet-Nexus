📖 How to use Skynet Nexus

Skynet Nexus is a news platform for the whole family — parents, kids, and teachers. Not doomscrolling: every story is built for reading together, understanding it, and talking about it. Here's how each of you uses it.

📰 Read→ 🧠 Understand→ 💬 Discuss→ 🏆 Earn
👨‍👩‍👧 Parents 🧒 Kids 🏫 Teachers

👨‍👩‍👧 Parents

You run the household newsroom. Set up profiles, co-read, and watch the XP roll in.

1Set up your family

Sign up as a Parent, then add a kid profile for each child from your profile page — name, age, avatar, and their favorite correspondent art style: Humans, Animals, or Skynet Agents. The whole site re-skins to their pick.

2Co-read with the guided journey

Every article walks you through four steps: the story, the Kid Take (rewritten at an age-8 reading level), Talk Together (discussion questions), and Words to Know (glossary). Read them in order, together — that's the core loop.

3Tap the XP buttons together

Each step has an XP button (10 / 5 / 15 / 5 XP). Tap them as you finish — your kid levels up from Rookie Reader to Editor-in-Chief, and you earn grown-up XP for co-reading (+10 per talk-together).

4Track progress, not screen time

Your profile page shows every kid's level, XP bar, reading streak, and badge collection (20 badges: milestones, streaks, channel explorers). It's proof of reading, not minutes stared.

5Make the discussion the point

The Talk Together questions are the heart of the platform. Fifteen XP says it all: the conversation is worth more than the click. Three editions drop daily (7:15 AM, 2:15 PM, 6:15 PM ET) so there's always something fresh to talk about.

🧒 Kids

You're the Junior Correspondent. Read stories, earn XP, climb the newsroom ranks.

1Pick your correspondents' look

In your kid profile, choose how your reporters appear: Humans, Animals, or Skynet Agents (robots!). Every byline portrait on the site changes to match.

2Read the day's editions

New front pages drop at 7:15 AM, 2:15 PM, and 6:15 PM Eastern. Explore 13 channels — Space, Robotics, Gaming, Music, AI, and more — each with its own correspondent.

3Finish steps, earn XP

Tap the XP buttons as you complete each reading step. Rack up XP to climb: Rookie Reader → Cub Reporter → Beat Reporter → Correspondent → Senior Correspondent → Editor → Editor-in-Chief.

4Collect badges & streaks

Earn 20 badges — finish your first story, read 3 days in a row (then 7, then 30!), become an explorer of every channel. Your progress card on the profile page shows it all.

5Join the class chat

If your teacher set up a classroom, open the Classroom page to see their discussions, share what you think, and earn 10 XP per discussion. Tap a correspondent's name on any story to read their bio and all their stories.

🏫 Teachers

Turn the news into a classroom activity — with live reading data on every student.

1Create your teacher account

Sign up as a Teacher, then create a student profile for each student — same system parents use for kids, no separate logins to manage.

2Build your classroom

Open the Classroom page (linked from your profile), create a class like "Ms. Rivera's 4th Grade," and add your students. One teacher can run multiple classes.

3Track reading live

The progress dashboard shows every student's XP, level, reading streak, stories finished, and badge count — sorted by XP. You'll know who's reading and who needs a nudge, without a single quiz to grade.

4Start article discussions

Pick an article (paste its URL) or just a topic, and open a threaded discussion for the class. Students reply, everyone sees everything classroom-style, and you moderate. You earn +20 XP for hosting.

5Make news a habit

Use case ideas: morning edition warm-up (read the 7:15 AM drop together), discussion prompts (one thread per week per class), channel challenges (which team earns the most explorer badges this month?). The editions do the content work — you do the teaching.
